1AC: The Meta-Review

  This paper explores how Chinese micro-drama creators on social media incorporat
              real-time audience feedback into their storytelling and production
              process. Based on all reviews and follow-up discussion, I recommend a
              R&R decision. I briefly summarize the strengths and main concerns.
              Please refer to the details for each review.

  For strengths, the topic is socially meaningful and relevant to HCI discussions on
              participatory media and creative labor (R1, R3); The paper is
              generally well-written, easy to follow, and organized clearly (R2,
              R3). All reviewers appreciate the clear visuals and effective workflow
              diagrams!

  For core concerns to be addressed:
  The contribution is not clearly stated to show novelty (R1, R2). Many findings are
              similar to prior work on online creators (e.g., YouTubers). The paper
              needs to clarify what’s new about micro-dramas and how its
              contributions differ from existing creator economy research.

  Some concepts/terminologies are still confusing and the scope is unclear (R1, R2).
              Does the paper focus on the micro-drama industry or screenwriting
              practices? Need to clarify the unclear distinctions between micro-
              dramas, short videos, and online content; also between content
              creators, screenwriters, and creative producers. Give clearer
              definition or context for broader audiences such as Chinese platforms
              (e.g., Douyin, Hongguo, iQiyi), “vertical screen micro-drama writers”,
              “MCN” etc.

  There are some missing details in method (R1, R2) such as author positionality,
              participant selection rationale, interview design, data analysis
              process, etc. Plus, traditional screenwriters’ inclusion is also not
              well justified.

  There is limited HCI literature (R1, R2). The paper mainly cites media and
              communication studies. Better to incorporate HCI research on creator
              economy, creative tension, platform affordances, and participatory
              design. Section 2.2 also should address issues like flat or
              stereotypical characters.

  The discussion section lacks depth (R1). Section 5 needs to link more back to
              existing HCI literature. R1 expected a deeper reflection on how
              audience feedback loops affect creative autonomy or platform design,
              so that Section 5.4 can offer more thoughtful rather than generic HCI
              design implications.

  There are also some minor issues like writing, organization, formatting, and
              narrative flow (R3, R1).

Review (Round 1)

  **I corrected expressions, writing structures, and grammars utilizing ChatGPT in
              this review, not feeding the paper to review it.

  Thank you for submitting the work to CHI 2026. The research is about how new
              emerging media content, micro-drama content, changes the workflow of
              screen-writers, by involving audience feedback in the context of
              Chinese market. To understand it, semi-structured interviews were
              conducted with multiple stakeholders including micro-drama
              scriptwriters. The interesting part here is, traditional screenwriting
              has been platformized by platform affordances that include comments,
              content format, likes, etc.

  1. Originality
  The result presents the landscape of micro-drama production. In particular, Fig 3
              shows how the video production workflow has been changed, compared to
              the traditional industries. However, the observed results are similar
              to those found in previous results. In the context of online content
              creators (such as YouTubers), audience-in-the-loop interactions are
              very common and general, so the findings are somewhat predictable (See
              Simpson et al., 2023's work). It is unclear how the author(s) work is
              different from others' work, in the context of screenwriting
              production. For me, it was a bit unclear to differentiate between
              micro-drama, short videos, online videos created by content creators,
              traditional videos, in terms of how creator workflow, the format of
              the content, stakeholders involved.

  2. Significance
  As noted in Section 1, some findings have already been highlighted in existing
              literature; however, the author(s) should thoroughly review what new
              insights this study offers. The work shows potential in emphasizing
              the 'narrative' aspect of micro-drama videos in a short-term format,
              which evolves based on audience feedback. This dynamic feedback loop
              hasn't been extensively studied in other works. Although the author(s)
              have attempted to address this, focusing on whether the audience loop
              influences the narrative format and whether it benefits video
              narratives would be beneficial. This focus could help platform
              designers make informed decisions about creating short-format videos
              with active audience involvement.

  Also, I am a bit lost if the author(s) work investigates 'micro drama industry' or
              'micro drama screen writing.' In the work, the terms are being used
              interchangeably; it would be nice to clearly describe in the
              introduction or related work. 'content creator' and 'screen writer' as
              well. My confusion is if the author(s) defines 'writing' as single
              writer's behaviors in writing micro drama or multiple practitioners'
              behaviors that engage in the workflow.

  Finally, in the current frame of the work, the author(s) focuses on micro-drama
              from the Chinese market and content production, which may limit its
              contribution if it doesn't generalize its findings to other forms of
              cultural production. This work could highlight its contribution on HCI
              by discussing other formats that use narratives or those that could be
              benefited from audience loop.

  3. Research Quality
  I appreciate that the author(s) interviewed participants from diverse backgrounds,
              including but not limited to screenwriters. However, it's important to
              ensure an equitable distribution of participants among practitioners.
              The author(s) also noted that most participants were recruited from
              their network. It would be beneficial for the author(s) to disclose
              their own positions and experiences within the industry and how they
              approached these contacts. Additionally, the interview methodology
              could be clearer, specifically regarding whether the design considered
              varying contexts and whether the questions were structured or open-
              ended.

  Also, Section 5 (Discussion) needs further development in relation to existing HCI
              research. I am particularly interested in the authors' insights on how
              the 'audience loop' influences micro-screen writing, including its
              advantages and disadvantages, within creative industries. Could this
              approach potentially undermine screenwriters' narratives or creative
              expression? My initial impression is that the production process,
              driven by audience feedback, might lead to skewed topics that cater to
              audience preferences. How does your perspective align or contrast with
              other scholars' work in this area? Should platforms enhance protective
              measures by modifying affordances, such as removing or altering
              comment and like sections? Section 5.4 should integrate these
              considerations alongside your findings and past studies.

  The research could incorporate HCI studies on the creator economy, creative
              writing, and creator-audience communication. It mainly references
              media and communication research. HCI researchers have previously
              examined topics like platform creative labor, creative tension, and
              multiplatform collaboration in CHI and CSCW papers focused on content
              creators.

  4. Miscellaneous
  - The section style is somewhat unusual in the format (e.g., 5.3 > 5.3.1). Also,
              this section could be moved in the order before the conclusion
              section.
  - As participants all have different roles in the micro-drama production, it would
              be more clear if the title is added when their quotes are described.
  - As all platforms (Hongguo, Douyin, Tencent, iQiyi) are not well known globally,
              authors should describe in the appendix about what these platforms are
              about and how they are different.

Review (Round 1)

  This paper examines the rising trend of Chinese micro-drama creation on social
              media, interviewing those in the “industry” to learn more about their
              creative, trend-driven, and audience-responsive processes. The authors
              argue that there is a novel type of workflow and story writing
              occurring in a triadic process among creators, audiences, and
              platforms that is highly responsive to moment-to-moment feedback. I
              find the paper to be a useful contribution to the HCI literature and
              very well-written.

  - I find the introduction succinct and easy-to-follow, but I did wonder about
              participant selection. I understand that the authors are arguing the
              roles are rather fluid, but the diversity of positions of these
              interviewees gives me some pause—can people from all of these
              positions effectively speak to your research questions? If so, how?
  - The term “vertical screen micro-drama writers” should be defined somewhere. I
              can assume that I know what you mean—but what makes these “vertical
              screen” writers different from other micro drama writers?
  - What does MCN stand for?
  - In section 2.2, I would like to see some engagement here with literature that
              touches on the potential issues with engaging flat, prototypical
              characters—things like feeding into stereotypes, reifying hegemonic
              assumptions, etc.
  - In Methods, it is confusing to say that the study focuses on three main research
              questions in this section because you only defined two official RQs in
              the introduction.
  - The inclusion of traditional screenwriters eventually made some sort of sense to
              me, but that integration remained unclear throughout the entirety of
              the study. I didn't realize that this was a comparative study until
              arriving at the "Participant Selection" section, and even then, I felt
              very unsure why/how/to what extend traditional screenwriters were
              included int the study. Foregrounding this more and/or fully
              explaining this participant selection decision would be useful.
  - In Data Analysis, I am unclear on how the coding of interviews worked alongside
              the content analysis of the micro dramas, themselves—where, how, and
              why would that content analysis data show up? Did this content
              analysis affect the code book? If so, I am struggling to understand
              how. The authors also mention that NOT using content analysis is a
              limitation of the study, so there needs to be more clarity on these
              processes.
  - I found the findings and discussion to be really well-written and succinct; the
              figures helped my understanding of the content, as well!
